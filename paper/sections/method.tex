\chapter{Methods}
\label{chap:method}
In this section, we describe the two proposed methods 
for adjusting for unmeasured time-invariant confounding: the fixed-effect marginal 
structural model (FE-MSM) proposed by \citeA{blackwellEffectPoliticalAdvertising2024}
and the sequential Gaussian process latent variable model (SeqGPLVM) 
proposed by \citeA{hattSequentialDeconfoundingCausal2024} when it is used 
in conjunction with IPTW estimation of MSM (SeqGPLVM-MSM). We first describe the common set of
assumptions required by of these methods, and then go 
into the details of the steps of each method.

\section{Notation and setup}
\label{sec:notation}
Let $i=1, \ldots, N$ index the individuals in the study and let $t=0, \ldots, T$ 
index the time points at which measurements are taken. The treatment 
variable is denoted as $A_{it}$, which can take on values from a 
predefined set of treatment options. In this thesis, 
the treatments are assumed to be binary, so that $A_{it} \in \{0,1\}$. We adopt the convention that treatment 
is absent at the beginning of the study. Therefore, $A_{i0} = 0$ 
for all individuals. 
The single measured outcome variable at the end of the study is defined as $Y_{i}$. 
Observed covariates are denoted by $X_{it}$ and may include 
both time-invariant and time-varying covariates.
The history of treatment and covariates up to time $t$ is represented as
$\bar{A}_{it} = (A_{i1}, A_{i2}, \ldots, A_{it})$ and $\bar{X}_{it} = (X_{i1}, X_{i2}, \ldots, X_{it})$, 
respectively. The potential outcome under a specific treatment
regime $\bar{a}_T = (a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_T)$ is denoted by $Y_{i}(\bar{a}_T)$. Finally, we use $U_i$ 
to represent \emph{structural} unmeasured confounders that are time-invariant for individual $i$. The truncated treatment histories from are defined as the last $k$ treatment values, 
denoted as $\underline{A}_{i,T-k} = (A_{i,T-k+1}, A_{i,T-k+2}, \ldots, A_{i,T})$. Similarly, 
the truncated covariate histories are defined as 
$\underline{X}_{i,T-k} = (X_{i,T-k+1}, X_{i,T-k+2}, \ldots, X_{i,T})$. Based on the 
definition of the truncated treatment history, the truncated potential outcomes can be defined 
as $Y_{i}(\underline{a}_{T-k}) \equiv Y_{i}(\overline{A}_{i,T-k-1},\, \underline{a}_{i,T-k})$.
$\underline{a}_{T-k} = (a_{T-k+1}, a_{T-k+2}, \ldots, a_{T})$. This definition 
represents the potential outcome of manipulating the last $k$ treatment values, 
while leaving the earlier treatment values as observed. In this way, 
the earlier treatments before the last $k$ time points can be 
thought of as part of the baseline covariates. 

\paragraph{Target causal estimand.} To avoid the usual incildental parameters 
problem in the estimation of non-linear fixed effects models, 
\citeA{blackwellEffectPoliticalAdvertising2024} propose to focus on the average 
truncated potential outcome as the causal target of interest instead of the usual 
potential outcome under a full treatment intervention. Although, SeqGPLVM can be used for 
identification of a wider range of causal targets 
including $\mathbb{E}[Y_i(\bar{a}_{T})]$, to maintain comparability with FE-MSM, 
we also focus on the average truncated potential outcome, $\mathbb{E}[Y_i(\underline{a}_{T-k})]$, 
as the causal target of interest in this thesis.
