\chapter{Methods}
\label{chap:method}
In this section, we describe the two proposed methods 
for adjusting for unmeasured time-invariant confounding: the fixed-effect marginal 
structural model (FE-MSM) proposed by \citeA{blackwellEffectPoliticalAdvertising2024}
and the sequential Gaussian process latent variable model (SeqGPLVM) 
proposed by \citeA{hattSequentialDeconfoundingCausal2024} when it is used 
in conjunction with IPTW estimation of MSM (SeqGPLVM-MSM). We first describe the common set of
assumptions required by of these methods, and then go 
into the details of the steps of each method.

\section{Notation and problem setup}
\label{sec:notation}

We use uppercase letters for random quantities and the corresponding lowercase
letters for their realizations. Boldface denotes vectors or collections, and plain
letters denote scalars. Individuals are indexed by \(i \in \{1,\dots,N\}\) and time
by \(t \in \{1,\dots,T\}\). Let \(\mathbf{X}_{it} \in \mathbb{R}^D\) denote the observed covariate vector for
individual \(i\) at time \(t\), and let \(A_{it} \in \mathcal{A}_t\) denote the
treatment at time \(t\). In this thesis, treatments are assumed to be binary, so
that \(A_{it} \in \{0,1\}\), although most notation and derivations extend directly
to more general treatment spaces. We adopt the convention that treatment is absent at the
beginning of the study, so \(A_{i0}=0\) for all \(i\). The terminal outcome is denoted by \(Y_i\). 

For each time point \(t\), define the collections
$
\mathbf{X}_t := \{\mathbf{X}_{it}\}_{i=1}^N
$ and
$
\mathbf{A}_t := \{A_{it}\}_{i=1}^N.
$
The full covariate and treatment collections are
$
\mathbf{X} := \{\mathbf{X}_t\}_{t=1}^T 
$ and 
$
\mathbf{A} := \{\mathbf{A}_t\}_{t=1}^T,
$
with realizations \(\mathbf{x}\) and \(\mathbf{a}\), respectively. 
The treatment and covariate histories up to time \(t\) are written as
$
\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{it} := (A_{i1},\dots,A_{it})
$ and 
$
\bar{\mathbf{X}}_{it} := (\mathbf{X}_{i1},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{it}).
$
For a treatment regime \(\bar{\mathbf{a}}_T = (a_1,\dots,a_T)\), the corresponding potential
outcome is denoted by \(Y_i(\bar{\mathbf{a}}_T)\).

We use \(\mathbf{U}_i \in \mathbb{R}^C\) to denote the structural unmeasured confounder for
individual \(i\), assumed to be time-invariant with dimension \(C\). In the latent-variable formulation,
we write \(\mathbf{Z} := \{\mathbf{Z}_i\}_{i=1}^N\) for the collection of
time-invariant latent variables, where \(\mathbf{Z}_i \in \mathbb{R}^J\) and \(J\)
is the latent dimension. The truncated treatment and covariate histories over the last \(k\) periods are
defined as
\[
\underline{\mathbf{A}}_{i,T-k} := (A_{i,T-k+1},\dots,A_{iT}),
\qquad
\underline{\mathbf{X}}_{i,T-k} := (\mathbf{X}_{i,T-k+1},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{iT}).
\]
For \(\underline{\mathbf{a}}_{T-k} = (a_{T-k+1},\dots,a_T)\), the corresponding truncated
potential outcome is defined by
\[
Y_i(\underline{\mathbf{a}}_{T-k})
\equiv
Y_i(\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i,T-k-1},\,\underline{\mathbf{a}}_{T-k}),
\]
that is, the last \(k\) treatment values are intervened on while the earlier
treatment history is left as observed. 

Finally, in context of SeqGPLVM, \(\boldsymbol{\gamma}\) denotes generative 
hyperparameters and \(\boldsymbol{\psi}\) variational hyperparameters. 
Generative hyperparameters are 
those that define how the data is generated and enter the prior and likelihood, e.g., 
the kernel lengthscale and variance in GPs, while variational hyperparameters are those 
that define the variational family, e.g., the mean and variance in Gaussian variational 
distributions.

\paragraph{Target causal estimand.} To avoid the usual incildental parameters 
problem in the estimation of non-linear fixed effects models, FE-MSM 
is limited to estimating only the average truncated potential outcome. 
Although, the sequential deconfounding approach and SeqGPLVM can be used for 
identification and estimation of a wider range of causal targets 
including $\mathbb{E}[Y_i(\bar{\mathbf{a}}_{T})]$, to maintain comparability with FE-MSM, 
we also focus on the average truncated potential outcome, $\mathbb{E}[Y_i(\underline{\mathbf{a}}_{T-k})]$, 
as the causal target of interest in this thesis.
