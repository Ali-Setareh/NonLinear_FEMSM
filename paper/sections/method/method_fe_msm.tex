\section{FE-MSM}
\label{sec:fe-msm}
FE-MSM consists of two stages: (i) a treatment model that 
estimates the propensity scores, conditioning on both observed 
covariates and individual fixed effects that capture the 
unmeasured time-invariant confounding, 
and (ii) a marginal structural model that uses the 
estimated propensity scores in stage one to adjust for 
confounding using IPTW. FE-MSM can be seen as a very direct way of 
bringing back the sequential 
ignorability assumption where we suspect of unmeasured time-invariant 
confounding. The fixed effect acts as a unit specific 
offset in the propensity score model, capturing all 
the time-invariant unmeasured confounding. More formally, the identification of 
the causal effect of interest relies on the following assumption:
\begin{idassumpFE}[Unit-specific ignorability]\label{ass:FE-unit-specific-ignor}
    Let $\alpha_i$ be a time-invariant unmeasured unidimensional random variable, For 
    all $i$, $t$ and $\bar{a}_t$,
    \[
    \{ Y_{i}(\bar{a}_{T}),\, \underbar{X}_{i,t+1}(\bar a_t) \}
\;\perp\!\!\!\perp\;
A_{it}
\;\big|\;
\bar{X}_{it},\,
\bar A_{i,t-1} = \bar a_{t-1},\,
\alpha_i. \]

\end{idassumpFE}

Assumption \ref{ass:FE-unit-specific-ignor} states that conditioned on 
the past treatment and covariate history, as well as 
the unmeasured fixed effect $\alpha_i$, 
the treatment assignment at time $t$ is independent of the future 
potential outcomes and covariates. Under this assumption along with 
the assumptions \ref{ass:C-consistency} to \ref{ass:C-positivity}, the truncated potential 
outcome $\mathbb{E}[Y_i(\underline{a}_{T-k})]$ can be nonparametrically identified as follows

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:truncated_potential_outcome_id}
    \mathbb{E}\bigl[Y_i(\underbar{a}_{T-k})\bigr]
    =
    \mathbb{E}\left[
    \frac{\mathbf{1}\bigl(\underbar{A}_{i,T-k} = \underbar{a}_{T-k}\bigr)\, Y_i}
    {\prod_{t=T-k}^{T} \pi_{it}^{a_t} (1 - \pi_{it})^{1 - a_t}}
    \right].
\end{equation}
Where $\pi_{it} = \mathbb{P}(A_{it} = 1 \mid \bar X_{it}, \bar A_{i,t-1}, \alpha_i)$. 
In practice, a parametric model such as  
$
\pi_{it}(\bar{x}_{it}, \bar{a}_{i,t-1}; \beta_0,\alpha_{i0})
=
\mathbb{P}\!\left(
A_{it}=1 \mid X_{it}=\bar{x}_{it},\; \bar{A}_{i,t-1}=\bar{a}_{i,t-1}; \beta_0, \alpha_{i0}
\right)$, 
is specified; where $\beta_0$ and $\alpha_{i0}$ are the true $k \times 1$ parameter vector and 
the unit-specific fixed effect respectively. 
This model is estimated via a
conditional maximum likelihood estimator. 
The log-likelihood function for the treatment model can be written as 
\[
\ell_{it}(\beta,\alpha_{i})=A_{it}\log \pi_{it}(\beta,\alpha_{i}) + 
(1-A_{it})\log\{1-\pi_{it}(\beta,\alpha_{i})\}.
\]
Let $\alpha_0 = (\alpha_{i0}, \ldots, \alpha_{iN})$, then $\beta_0$ and $\alpha_0$ 
are the solutions to the following optimization problem: 
\[ 
(\beta_0, \alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_N)
= \arg\max_{\beta,\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{t=1}^{T}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\ell_{it}(\beta,\alpha_i)\mid \alpha_i\right].
\]
The estimates $\hat\alpha = (\hat\alpha_1, \ldots, \hat\alpha_N)$ and $\hat\beta$ are 
obtained by solving the finite-sample analog of this optimization problem. 

Having obtained the estimates of the propensity scores, the weights for the IPTW 
estimation of the marginal structural model are constructed as
\begin{equation}
\widehat{W}_i(\hat\beta,\hat\alpha_i)=\prod_{t=T-K}^{T}
\left(\frac{1}{\pi_{it}(\hat\beta,\hat\alpha_i)}\right)^{A_{it}}
\left(\frac{1}{1-\pi_{it}(\hat\beta,\hat\alpha_i)}\right)^{1-A_{it}}
\label{eq:iptw_weights}
\end{equation}
where only the last $K$ time points are included in the product. However, the 
propensity score model is estimated using the full treatment history. 

If a marginal structural model is specified as $\mathbb{E}[Y_i(\underline{a}_{T-k})] = m(\underline{a}_{T-k}; \gamma_0)$,
let 
\[
\Psi_N(\gamma)
:=
\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N}
\widehat{W}_i(\hat\beta, \hat\alpha_i)\,
h\!\left(\underbar{A}_{i,T-k}\right)\,
\left[
Y_i - m\!\left(\underbar{A}_{i,T-k}; \gamma \right)
\right].
\]
Then, the estimator $\hat{\gamma}$ is defined as a solution to the sample
estimating equation
\[
\Psi_N(\gamma)=0.
\]
In this equation $h(\cdot)$ is a user-specified function of the treatment history. 
If the marginal structural model is specified as a linear model, a common choice is 
to set $h(\underbar{A}_{i,T-k}) = \underbar{A}_{i,T-k}$. Then the 
parameters of the marginal structural model can be estimated via a weighted 
least squares regression of $Y_i$ on $\underbar{A}_{i,T-k}$ using the weights 
defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:iptw_weights}. The consistency of the $\hat{\gamma}$ relies on the 
correct specification of the propensity score 
model up to the unmeasured fixed effect $\alpha_i$. 

\begin{modassumpFE}[Correct specification of the parametric component of the propensity score model]
\label{ass:ps-correct-spec}
Suppose there exists a finite-dimensional parameter vector $\beta_0$ and unit-specific effects
$\alpha_{i0}$ such that, for all $i$ and $t$,
\[
\mathbb{P}(A_{it}=1 \mid X_{it}, A_{i,t-1}, \alpha_i)
=
\pi_{it}(X_{it}, A_{i,t-1}; \beta_0, \alpha_{i0})
\qquad \text{a.s.}
\]
That is, conditional on the unit-specific effect $\alpha_i$, the treatment assignment mechanism
is correctly specified as a member of the posited parametric family in $(X_{it},A_{i,t-1})$.
\end{modassumpFE}

This assumption only requires the propensity score to be correctly specified up to the 
unit-specific effect $\alpha_i$ and it does not impose any assumptions on the 
relationship between $\alpha_i$ and the observed covariates. Based on this assumption and 
other regularity assumptions, \citeA{blackwellEffectPoliticalAdvertising2024} showed in 
Theorem 1 that 
\[
\sqrt{N}\,(\widehat{\gamma}-\gamma_{0})
\overset{d}{\to}
N(0, V_{\gamma_0}),
\]
where $V_{\gamma_0}$ is a sandwich-type asymptotic variance that can be consistently estimated using the sample analog of the sandwich formula.
